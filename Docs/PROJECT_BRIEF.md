# Project brief

## Intended experience

Build a colony on a procedural planet, grow industry, and respond to the environmental and social cost of expansion through construction and policy decisions.

## Supplies and growth (current rules, 2026-10-29)

Hover **FOOD / RESERVE [?]** or **WATER / RESERVE [?]** for the colony reserve outlook. After one full simulated month, the outlook compares farm food actually delivered or pump water actually added over the last 150 simulated seconds with current monthly demand. It shows the net rate and estimates time to empty when inflow falls short and stock remains. The estimate assumes those rates continue. The ribbon still reports how long stored supplies alone cover current demand. Orbital rations increase stored food and are excluded from the measured inflow. Pause freezes observation. New and loaded colonies start a fresh observation window using their current reserves.

The top ribbon shows **FOOD**, **WATER**, **BUILD SUPPLIES**, population, workers and **health (fixed at 100%)**. Food/water months are stored reserve divided by current population demand, excluding future deliveries/production. Build supply months use recent gross component consumption (farm operation, construction and improvements); **NO DEMAND** means no recent use. Counts remain visible, with plasteel shown separately. Construction is discretionary, so its displayed months are an observed-use estimate rather than a guaranteed build budget.

**Founding route:** land -> one connected groundwater pump -> one connected farm -> iron mine + stone quarry + biomass harvester -> Manufactorum on its default components recipe. One full pump makes 180 water/month; 6,000 crew use 97.5/month. One full founding farm makes 6,000 food/month using one component and six reclaimed-waste fertilizer. The ship supplies components to connected recipe buffers; its crew supply reclaimed waste. This buys time to establish renewable construction supplies. Connected consumers receive ingredients before surplus is stored on the ship. Additional generation is needed as the industry outgrows the 18 MW reactor. Smelter/Works is an optional longer components chain, not a prerequisite for the Manufactorum.

Growth responds to food reserve, water reserve, housing, environmental habitability and fixed population health. Full growth toward available healthy housing requires three months of food and one month of water. Lower reserves reduce positive growth continuously; an empty essential reserve causes decline. Growth uses a 0.0002/second response, so a healthy 6,000-person colony gains about 118 people in its first month rather than filling 4,000 berths in seconds. Population consumption and capacity must continue to be monitored as it grows.

**Credits are retired.** New buildings spend physical components/plasteel. Residential development and adaptive improvements spend components as work progresses, with historical project price/paid units mapped at 100 old units to one component. Already-paid work and saved progress are retained. No old credit balance is converted to free cargo. Snapshot currency/output/upkeep fields remain only for save compatibility and do not fund or drain gameplay; existing material inventories and zero reserves retain their exact values. Discovery recovery and historical currency-bound material cargo now yield physical supplies, with recovered overflow respecting real inventory reservations. Ship-bound cargo can be saved while travelling and requires a real saved shared destination. Existing IDs, GUIDs, schema 16 and enabled playable scene remain stable; runtime HUD wiring covers these changes. Placement reports missing supplies, unsuitable deposits/coastlines, mineral reservations and blocked ground. Catalog cards show costs and availability; the guide explains the short raw-material chain.

## Manufactorum expansion (2026-10-29)

Manufactorums have separate **EXPAND** and **TECH TIER** controls in their production panel; the inspector also offers EXPAND. Warehouse growth is player-approved with no bay-count, radius or technology-tier cap. The connected frontier searches around all existing bays, trying another direction when obstructed. Terrain, water, excavation safety, buildings, farm/residential territory, pending reservations and the dozer can block expansion. Pending sites also block new buildings.

Each bay costs **20 components + 2 plasteel**, paid on approval, and takes **60 simulated seconds**. Suspension or blocked ground preserves the paid project and work. Completion adds **100% of founding throughput, jobs, nominal upkeep and power demand**. Each bay also consumes **one physical component per fully staffed/powered month**. When that upkeep cannot be funded, annex throughput idles while the founding capacity can operate. Active power/job demand remains. Products share work equally and retain their recipe inputs and configured stock buffers. Expansion does not unlock recipes or enlarge buffers.

Tier II costs **12 components + 6 advanced plasteel** and unlocks plasteel/synthetic crystal; Tier III costs **24 components + 12 plasteel** and unlocks advanced mineral nutrients. Components, refined metal and compost are available at Tier I. Tiers add no capacity; warehouse expansion remains available at Tier III. Historical selected recipes keep operating on load; selecting a new advanced recipe requires its tier.

Optional `operation.manufactorum` data within **schema 16** saves completed bays, inherited historical capacity, the prepaid reservation and exact work. Old saves receive no supplies and retain their tier/output bonus. Historical pending generic improvements retain paid progress and their original remaining component cost, completing their original tier/capacity bonus once. Further generic improvements do not start automatically. IDs, existing GUIDs and Build Settings are preserved; the playable scene attaches the controller, visuals and UI at runtime.

Main warehouse halls are about **288 x 180 m**, with stacks, gantries, conveyors, fans, buttresses, loading doors and heavy service equipment. Core galleries and adjoining aprons join the complex. They share the project's original generated photographic steel, roofing, stone and brass atlas for a Warhammer-inspired gothic industrial appearance; these are procedural buildings using photographic surfaces, rather than imported official artwork. Gantry travel, fan rotation and conveyor pallets follow simulation time and staffing/power, stopping on pause or suspension. Manual, standalone and sustained performance acceptance are separate from automated checks.

## Current milestone

### Optional live founding objectives (2026-10-29)

The post-landing guide offers optional live objectives. BEGIN enables a compact non-pausing tracker; NOT NOW declines it. COLONY GUIDE can enable/hide the tracker and revisit all facility instructions. The guide itself still pauses while open. Its overview and final page show actual saved milestones, rather than calling a browsed reference list complete.

Milestones recognise operating pump output, farm food cargo actually delivered into the shared reserve, and actual recipe production of construction components (including the alternative Works route). Placing buildings, starting stock, recovery rewards and cargo transfers do not count as manufacturing. Milestones can be earned in any order; the next incomplete one explains workers, power, ingredients and connectivity. They are historical achievements, not promises that a facility remains active.

After all three milestones, observe one full 150-second simulated month. Pump output must cover population water demand, delivered farm food must cover food demand, and manufactured components must cover all component spending, including construction and improvements. Food/water must remain above zero. Failed months restart observation; pause freezes it. The observation includes real cargo timing and does not claim indefinite independence from finite deposits. Developer infinite-supply facilities cannot qualify the balance window.

Completing the balanced month reveals a narrative orbital cargo canister containing **20 physical construction components**. The guide's **RECOVER CANISTER CONTENTS** action transfers into available ship/facility storage, respecting incoming cargo reservations. Unstored contents remain recoverable; recovered amounts never count as production. Optional additive `industry.founding` struct data within schema 16 preserves exact milestones, current month totals, between-tick component spending, tracker preference and one-time reward recovery. Missing old-save data starts empty and awards nothing; saves never refill stocks. IDs, existing GUIDs and enabled scene remain unchanged. Runtime HUD/simulation wiring supplies the feature in `WH30K_VS01`.

One coherent ten-minute session: begin a world, construct a useful building, see its economic/environmental effects, make a consequential discovery or mining decision, save, and reload with the same colony and pending decision state. Recurring industrial policy transmissions are on hold for later review.

## Visual direction and scale

Local vegetation follows the viewed surface rather than the point beneath the camera. At wider colony views it samples a wider area while keeping the existing resident-tree cap and seed/cell identities; local ecology remains enabled to 12 km altitude. The orbiting Explorator keeps its actual 3 km scale with a cheaper silhouette at distant zoom; the landed tender keeps its actual 500 m scale. The surface-to-orbit camera transition rises overhead while keeping the viewed surface centred, so zooming out does not steer either vessel out of frame. At whole-planet distances each vessel naturally becomes subpixel; no oversized orbital icon is substituted.

Gothic industrial architecture: weathered limestone and forged iron, oxide-red roofs, aged brass, buttresses, pointed windows and warm lumen strips. Each building has a distinct mesh silhouette. Factories have furnace stacks and roof bays; hab blocks have stacked apartments and spires; outposts are fortified keeps; scrubbers have paired filter towers, intake vanes and teal status lights. Core buildings are connected by service causeways. Built structures and placement previews share the same geometry, constrained by their placement footprints, with seven shared material groups per building.

Every building, residential design, adaptive addition and service gallery shares a generated photographic surface atlas: limestone ashlar, oxidised steel, red standing-seam roofing and tarnished brass. A built-in Standard surface shader maps it along building-local axes in metres, including parent scale, with shallow luminance-derived relief, rough corrosion, subdued metallic highlights and light dust on upward surfaces. Large solid volumes have small physical edge chamfers; tanks/stacks have smoother round profiles. Residential design colours tint the photographic surfaces, while placement/construction ghosts retain their UI colours. The Resources material explicitly retains the shader and atlas for player builds. This is a material/geometry realism pass on modular architecture; Unity shader import, actual rendering and performance acceptance remain pending activation of the Editor. See [Building appearance](BUILDING_APPEARANCE.md) for assets, provenance and review commands.

New planets have a **3,185,500-metre radius** and **6,371 km diameter**, half Earth's mean radius/diameter ([NASA reference](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html)). One Unity unit remains one metre: the Explorator stays 3 km long, the landing tender is 500 m, and buildings retain their existing dimensions. Terrain relief stays in kilometres rather than growing in proportion to the globe; large-world recipes use six times the historical 6 km planet's relief, with clouds at 12 km. Existing saves retain their exact stored radius, terrain recipe, colony positions and grid resolution. Surface picking uses double-precision sphere intersection, local distances avoid float angle rounding, and survey/dozer/construction/save limits support the larger sphere. Climate biome shading, spherical oceans, layered clouds, atmospheric scattering, polar ice, shadows and an orbital star field establish the planetary view. Ocean rendering follows the terrain patches across camera zoom levels. The shoreline masks the shallowest 2.5 m of sampled water depth and tapers wave displacement toward land to reduce flicker. Atmospheric haze fades distant ground and scales with altitude; far terrain roots use lower mesh density, while local patches retain their detailed geometry and bounded split work. F frames the active colony or landing tender; Home shows the hemisphere containing it. Schema-9 saves store the radius and complete terrain recipe. Older saves retain their coastline mapping, building IDs and grid cells; original 3,000-unit settlement anchors retain the earlier migration onto the 12,000-unit legacy sphere.

Each new expedition derives a saved, seed-stable planetary profile spanning temperate continental, oceanic, arid, cold, rugged and warm humid worlds. Profiles vary the terrain sampler, continental coverage, mountain uplift, roughness, temperature and moisture together to create corresponding landforms and biome balances. Explicitly customized terrain recipes and loaded worlds remain unchanged; the complete selected recipe is persisted with the planet.

Terrain plates have seed-stable angular motion and buoyancy. Relative plate motion classifies convergent, divergent and transform boundaries: continental collision drives mountain belts, ocean-continent subduction forms volcanic arcs and trenches, divergence creates rift valleys and shoulders, and shear creates fault scarps. The shared spherical height sampler applies these features to geographic, hybrid and fractal terrain while keeping boundary transitions continuous across cube faces and LOD patches. This is a deterministic procedural approximation, not a time-stepped geophysics simulation.

Workers are distributed proportionally across active jobs. Housing, essential reserves, health and habitability constrain population growth. Scrubbers need staff and clean air/water. Factories reclaim worker waste and are optional; they do not provide currency income. Physical recipe inputs and power constrain production.

Habitability begins to fall above 600 CO2, below 19.5 oxygen, above 10 water pollution, or beyond a two-degree temperature offset. It is bounded between 25% and 100% and affects population targets, staffing, and production. Safety choices reduce future harmful emissions as well as cleaning existing damage; overdrive raises future emissions. Cleanup benefits are not multiplied by industrial policy.

## Command interface, navigation and time

**Resource deposits pane:** open **RESOURCE DEPOSITS** in the logistics column. Each assigned extraction source appears once with its survey ID/material, starting reserve, remaining reserve, actual extraction per month, and expected remaining life in months. Multiple extractors using one source contribute to that source's measured removal. Rates use the last 30 simulated seconds (one fifth of a month); life is remaining reserve divided by that monthly rate, so staffing, power, full buffers and excavation restrictions affect the forecast. Idle assigned sources remain listed with “No recent extraction”; exhausted mineral sources show “EXHAUSTED”. These are observed-use forecasts, not promises of future output. Groundwater/desalination have no finite surface deposit and do not appear.

The pane does not pause time. Click a row to focus its actual surface direction and enable its material overlay. **PIN TO HUD** docks the pane beside the left column and retains it through selection/navigation; **UNPIN FROM HUD** returns it to a floating window, which closes after visiting a source or selecting another building. X closes either mode; Escape closes the floating pane. Mouse-wheel scrolling covers longer lists. Pin preference persists locally between sessions. Existing scene wiring builds this UI at runtime, with no scene GUID or building-ID changes.

Starting reserves are captured before extraction and saved in an additive, optional `initialVolume` deposit field within schema 16. Older saves keep exact remaining reserves; solid deposits recover their start from saved geological capacity. Historical biomass/fuel saves lack enough information to recover the original start reliably and show **UNKNOWN** until a new expedition. No reserve is refilled. Usage history restarts on load and gathers fresh observations; this is stated by the pane's recent-extraction labels.


Hover **BUILD SUPPLIES**, **Population** or **Workers / Health** for live reserve and growth explanations, including the current supported population target and monthly trend. The panel explains months arithmetic, recent construction usage, health, housing and current environmental stress with corrective actions. It refreshes while open and rebuilds from saves.

The command HUD uses a compact industrial-control layout inspired by *Captain of Industry*: a top command ribbon for food/water/build supply coverage, population, workforce/health and calendar/speed/save controls; a stacked planetary-vitals and logistics column on the left; a construction/category dock along the bottom; and a contextual structure inspector on the right. Its Warhammer 40,000 Mechanicus treatment uses a slate instrument-panel base, brass labels, oxide-red section markers and technical directorate naming. Developer controls are hidden behind F12 in the editor. The camera opens in free orbit, supports WASD navigation, middle-drag surface panning and right-drag rotation, and can cross from orbital to local views by zooming. A separate camera draws distant celestial scenery and receives no mouse events; the gameplay camera retains resource-marker clicks and local selection. The saved prototype explicitly includes its required ResearchSystem component. F is an optional colony shortcut; selecting a building never recenters the camera.

Architecture transforms retain the 0.2 scale relative to the first visual pass. A newly constructed Residential Block occupies about 16 metres of height at the core scale; other building dimensions remain unchanged. New construction uses a sparse, radius-scaled cube grid (135,915 cells per face on the default world, retaining approximately the former 256-cell/6 km grid's local spacing); only nearby cells are evaluated. Placement remains free-direction. Older saves retain their stored resolution. Core spacing and causeways use the same smaller scale.

The shared simulation clock advances one year per 1,800 real seconds at 1x, with twelve 150-second months and thirty days per month. Pause and 1x/3x/10x affect calendar, settlement and event progression together. Real play time counts unscaled time while a colony is active, including planning pauses. Decisions pause simulated time without locking camera/UI interaction. Version-4 saves include both elapsed times, speed/pause and each building's tier/focus/offline state; older saves default to balanced active buildings and a fresh clock.

Every core and player-built structure has its own operations state. Focus is free to change. Residential blocks offer high-density housing or civil administration; factories offer surge or clean production; utilities offer deep purification or economy cycles; outposts offer logistics or residential support. Focus tradeoffs alter actual simulation values. Suspension pauses construction and removes operating effects.

Nonresidential adaptive improvements retain a maximum of tier III and add 25% capacity/jobs and 15% upkeep per tier. Automatic milestones require 50% staffing for 180 seconds per tier; players may approve manually. Work takes 60 simulation seconds per tier, spending 1.5/1.8/2.1 components per tier for vertical/balanced/radial plans. Symmetric radial campuses and their covered galleries, or building-specific crowns, remain the nonresidential presentation. Shared gallery coordinates and box face winding have been corrected.

After a new landing, the player can begin an optional colony-establishment guide or dismiss it. The guide explains the ship's initial food, berths and reactor, then walks through the founding needs and every player-buildable facility, marking essential services versus expansion options. Each facility page explains its operation, inputs or placement constraints, colony role and construction category. **BUILD THIS** selects the matching catalog page and begins placement. The guide pauses simulation while open, can be reopened from the command bar, and reports when a facility is already present. Its page position is session UI state and does not change the save schema.
## Bulldozing and earthworks

The construction deck exposes **BULLDOZE** and **EARTHWORKS**. Bulldoze selects a player-built facility, marks its footprint red and requires the in-game **REMOVE BUILDING / NO REFUND** button. Removal includes all residential modules, jobs, housing, local inventory and linked cargo; other facilities and cargo are remapped immediately, even when paused. The founding ship and legacy core facilities remain protected under the existing mining safety rule. No refund is granted.

A free Praetor heavy tracked dozer parks on clear land near the colony. Its roughly 28 × 15 × 33 metre procedural silhouette includes paired tracks, animated road wheels, an armoured cab and a wide grading blade. Click it to select it, or open Earthworks and use **LOCATE**. In **MOVE**, left-click a destination. Travel follows the sphere at 9 metres per simulation second and stops before water, steep slopes, buildings or reserved development. Use intermediate clicked waypoints around obstructions; automatic ground route finding is outside this slice. New building placement and residential development avoid the vehicle. A fallback **DEPLOY DOZER** control retries parking if the founding site offers no safe space.

The separate engineering HUD and surface brush use cyan for **FLATTEN**, amber for **RAISE**, violet for **LOWER**, green for movement, and red for demolition or invalid terrain. Radius cycles through 16/24/40/64 m; raise/lower steps through 2/4/8 m. Click a point to issue one command. The dozer moves into range, spends four simulation seconds working, then applies the command once. Flatten retains the clicked centre's radial grade, with a feathered perimeter; raise/lower preserve local relief. Pause, speed controls and decision pauses affect movement and work together. **STOP DOZER** cancels pending work; leaving the HUD keeps the current command running. Commands and deployment are free in this prototype.

Earthworks update the shared terrain heightfield used by LOD meshes, picking, camera clearance and construction. Worked areas clear local decorative vegetation. Brushes protect building/reserved development footprints and mineral body/excavation extents, require sampled dry land, limit cumulative grade change to ±80 m, and cannot lower below the waterline. A world retains at most 512 ordered commands; a spatial index bounds sampling to nearby edits. Terrain changes are persistent and have no undo in this slice.

Schema 10 extends schema 9 with the dozer's direction/facing, destination, pending work and ordered terrain commands. It replays terrain before restoring foundations and geology. Saves from schemas 0–9 retain their existing migration, terrain recipe, IDs, positions and economy, start without terrain edits, and receive a parked dozer where space permits. Invalid earthworks state is rejected before replacing an active session. Runtime bootstrap wiring supplies the controller and HUD in the existing enabled `WH30K_VS01` scene.

## Residential development

**Residential Block** keeps the internal ID `HabBlock` and replaces residential rings/crowns with incremental candidate-driven modules. The configurable Resources JSON library contains twenty worker, twenty administration and twenty leadership designs with distinct occupied footprints/height profiles, capacity, role, entrances, roof/lift support, cost, work and environmental preferences. A mixed founding residence and a small connecting service gallery are additional pieces. Buildings develop through matched floor entrances, infill galleries and individually supported upper floors, respecting terrain, neighbouring occupied volumes, causeways and excavation envelopes. New ground wings prefer a two-tile gap (roughly 8–10 m at current building scales), crossed by a narrow, terrain-supported covered gallery. One-tile gaps or compact infill remain options when the site is constrained; individual house volumes also maintain a configurable two-metre clearance from other facilities. Ground additions receive their own foundations; upper roofs reconcile with completed additions. All sixty designs now have explicit, distinct wall/roof/trim palettes, five roof profiles and four facade treatments; repeated copies have restrained saved-serial colour variation. Selection discourages repeating a design when alternatives fit. Stone ceilings and roof finishes occupy separate planes to avoid surface speckling.

Each block mixes ground wings and supported upper floors through successive additions. Balanced targets roughly equal numbers; Upward targets roughly two upper additions per ground addition, and Outward the inverse. Geometry and demand can override that target. Upper floors can develop above any completed load-bearing wing, so preferences do not lock a block to one direction. Each completed habitation addition increases actual internal housing once; the project inspector shows its forthcoming capacity increase.

Classes partition the existing population 78%/18%/4%, without creating another workforce or duplicating jobs. Construction uses physical components: full funding is needed to approve a new project; payment follows work and shortages pause it. Actual staffing, connected power and nearby active utilities affect work. Actual global habitability, CO2, water pollution and temperature influence enclosed/open module selection. No local pollution, wind or noise field is invented.

Select a block/module and scroll its inspector for class provision/demand, project cost/paid work/waiting reason, preferred option and module role/connections. Toggle Automatic/Manual, adjust Balanced/Upward/Outward preference, approve a project, or suspend/resume. Whole Block Details clears individual module detail. A continuous pale-yellow outline follows completed tiles and courts; faint dashed suggestions and supported upward ghosts share construction validation. Brighter yellow is preferred; amber is unfunded. At most five suggestions appear and claim no territory. Construction remains stationary at its planned dimensions with an amber ghost/work plane. If another building or terrain change obstructs an approved project, it automatically selects another valid site for that same design, preserving the quoted cost, paid supply value and work. This also applies when current demand has fallen to zero. If no alternative fits, the project waits with its investment intact. Existing completed placements remain saved in place.

Residential state is additive within schema-9 operations: identity/seed/sequence, ordered stable module IDs and ownership, placements/connections, class/capacity, completed state, settings, locked project cost/duration and paid/remaining work. Legacy completed tiers consolidate into the founding footprint with their original economic effects; old rings are not also rendered. Pending historical work retains its price and paid progress, waits for a legal replacement site and grants its original tier once, without also counting the replacement module's housing. This intentionally changes legacy visual layouts to avoid unvalidated overlaps. Searches and displays are bounded, defaulting to 32 pieces and 12 tiles/levels. ## Geography and physical resource chains

New worlds use terrain recipe 3: interchangeable warped fractal/ridged terrain, spherical plate-region geography, or geographic/fractal hybrid with erosion-inspired valleys. Defaults favor broad landforms, softened mountain uplift and low-amplitude surface detail; mountain chains remain distinct without putting fine noise over every plain. All LODs, placement and prospecting sample the same immutable seeded height generator. Latitude, elevation, regional temperature/moisture and slope blend grassland, forest, desert, rocky highlands, tundra and snow/ice. Coverage controls are biases rather than guaranteed area percentages. Construction areas are never automatically flattened. Recipes 0–2 remain unchanged for established saves; start a new expedition to preview the revised recipe. Close views retain deterministic batched vegetation and rocks; existing waves use simulation time with vertex swells, dynamic normals, shallow foam and a local detailed water patch.

**WH30K > Terrain Comparison** stages generation controls, three fixed seeds, elevation/slope/biome overlays and mesh/timing counters. Explicit regeneration previews terrain in Scene view, or starts a temporary Play Mode world with a previous-colony restore control. Optional rock/soil/grass/snow albedo slots on `PlanetTerrain` use slope-aware, two-scale triplanar blending; enable assigned textures on the material after supplying assets. Defaults remain procedural art. `PlanetBootstrap` → `LODPlanet` is the playable generation path; `PlanetGenerator` belongs only to the historical, unbuilt SampleScene.

A sun, two moons and a distant gas world provide a stylized solar scene. One visual day is 180 simulation seconds, deliberately slower than the five-second calendar day; moon orbits are also compressed. The sun rotates relative to the fixed surface coordinates, preserving all placement anchors while producing day/night lighting. These are presentation orbits without gravity, seasons or eclipse simulation.

New construction records a finite unit surface direction and ignores grid snapping. Support piles permit water and steep sites. Costs, non-overlap and the building limit still apply. Older grid placements retain their stored grid coordinates. Schema 5 includes these free directions and industry state.

The construction deck has Colony, Extraction, Power / Storage and Processing pages. Geological survey cycles Off/All and iron/copper/stone/biomass/crystal/promethium filters, colored pins and route lines. Visit Nearest Deposit targets a matching site without changing the colony shortcut. Prospecting is deterministic from seed, terrain recipe and landing region; elevations/moisture constrain mineral and biomass placement. Mineral bodies have finite seeded reserves, different grades, extents and orientations; biomass patches retain their 2,000-unit limit. Physical outcrops indicate locations without the overlay. New geology adds finite promethium seeps to dry lowlands while preserving the earlier deposit ordering for save compatibility.

The Frontier Survey Outpost reveals nearby planetary sites within a configurable 1,800 m surface radius. Apart from the small surface deposits recorded by new-expedition orbital scans, geological outcrops, resource pins, ancient ruins, abandoned Imperial installations, hazardous regions and a rare archaeotech cache stay hidden until covered by an active survey outpost. The discovery counter and clickable markers expose explored opportunities; selected deposits report finite reserves and use compatible extraction facilities. Ruins and installations offer deterministic cautious-investigation or rapid-salvage decisions through the event-choice UI. Outcomes use the existing stockpile/environment systems, are recorded once, and persist with discovered deposits and point-of-interest locations in schema-8 saves. Earlier saves remain loadable and start with new sites undiscovered.

Four discovery ScriptableObjects in `Resources/Discoveries` configure names, descriptions, choices, marker colors, reward multipliers, incident chance and environmental consequences. Imperial salvage is more valuable than common ruins, rare archaeotech has the largest return, and cautious vent containment improves planetary conditions. The Sites button opens a browser with previous/next navigation, locate controls and persistent expedition reports. Distinct procedural ruins, bastions, vents and reliquaries appear in local views. New worlds favor a few early expeditions near the landing region where terrain permits; saved site directions are preserved. Invalid or overlapping fallbacks are omitted rather than placed in the ocean.

The All / Routes overlay adds grey unknown and teal surveyed regional markers. Coverage uses 1,024 equal-area sphere samples, so its area percentage is approximate. Charted cells remain saved after survey outposts are suspended or removed. Coverage and recorded reward/incident details are additive schema-8 fields; earlier schema-8 saves omit them safely, and historical resolved sites remain locked against duplicate rewards.

Mines automatically select iron, copper or crystals from the nearest revealed live compatible deposit at placement; no mineral selection is required. Quarries extract stone and harvesters gather biomass. The chosen mineral uses the existing saved focus field, so loading preserves historical selections and exact reserves. Extractors must be placed within 250 metres of a revealed live compatible deposit. Smelters convert two ore plus one biomass into one metal. Fabrication works convert two metal, one stone and half a crystal into one component. Five staffed simulation seconds complete one cycle; tiers and industrial focuses modify throughput, upkeep and emissions. Each facility stores up to 120 of each material. No input is consumed unless the whole recipe and output storage are available.

Automatic atmospheric cargo haulers dispatch batches of up to three units to the closest suitable consumer, reserve incoming storage and deliver only on arrival. Historical core-bound components now remain physical construction supplies in shared storage. Vehicles follow surface-relative flight arcs rather than a road network. Deposits, local inventories, partial work and in-flight cargo persist; old saves initialize empty industrial stocks. The new industrial buildings also retain normal workforce/upkeep/environment effects. Machinery rotates only while operating and the simulation is running.

## Industrial production chains

Construction is paid in physical supplies. The Manufactorum turns iron ore, stone and biomass into manufactured components, which serve as basic construction supplies; plasteel is the advanced supply tier. Each buildable definition carries stable supply costs, and placement checks and consumes those supplies from facility inventories. Existing material indices and inventory save data are reused. In the Unity Editor, the Developer Supply Nexus is an unlimited construction source while active; suspend/resume it in the building inspector to switch the source off and on. The dev-only definition is omitted from player builds.

The resource inventory retains the original seven save indices and appends promethium and plasteel. It reports nine material totals, key-chain production/consumption rates, power supply/demand, workforce demand and cargo. The selected-building inspector shows its recipe, local stock, estimated output rate, efficiency, logistics and operating status. Energy is a capacity flow, not a stockpile. Legacy core utility/factory buildings provide the initial grid supply so starting industry can operate. Finite promethium deposits feed extractors and fuel generators; generators increase connected-component power and emit pollution. Refineries atomically consume two iron ore and one promethium to make plasteel; the configured Manufactorum consumes one iron ore, one stone and half a biomass to make one component. Cargo routing and the inspector follow these configured recipe inputs. Existing smelters and works retain their recipes and IDs. Mineral extraction throughput scales with a deposit's surveyed grade, while reserves remain finite.

Mining Facility, Promethium Extractor, Promethium Generator, Refinery, Manufactorum and Storage Depot are data-driven buildable assets. Their profiles define power demand/output, fuel, extraction, storage, recipes and operating environmental impact. Production uses deterministic five-second work cycles, global proportional staffing and proportional power within each connected network component. Missing inputs and full output stores leave recipe inputs untouched. New inventory slots and lifetime production/consumption totals plus recent rates are stored in schema-7 saves. Versions 0–6 retain their existing migration behavior and initialize newly introduced fuel/material state safely.

Industry facilities automatically form a surface-distance graph: nearby facilities connect directly, and Storage Depots extend link range and physically store surplus cargo. Connected facilities exchange finite cargo batches with pooled atmospheric haulers; disconnected clusters cannot exchange inventory or use the colony grid. Direct network links can be visualized with the logistics overlay. Network edges are rebuilt from saved building positions, so their connectivity is restored deterministically without a separate road-save format. Player-authored roads and vehicle path planning remain future work.

The Routes overlay builds its decorative graph over multiple frames, including while paused, with a HUD status while it is being prepared. Each frame evaluates at most 2,048 candidate pairs and generates at most 256 terrain-following segments. A finished graph is published together; hiding Routes cancels unfinished work, and construction, demolition, loading or terrain edits invalidate it. The display is limited to 65,536 segments (262,144 vertices), with an explicit HUD indication at the limit. This bounds presentation memory without limiting the actual power/cargo network or changing saved state.

Generator dispatch now retires only the load sustainable with each generator's actual fuel, staffing and elapsed time. Nearly exhausted generators leave their remaining load for the next generator, including trace fuel below 0.001 units. Idle generators spend no fuel when the ship or another generator already meets demand. Production and emission accounting use the actual fuel consumed. Farm simulation reuses its estate-operation scratch dictionary and scalar fertilizer snapshots rather than allocating these every simulation step. Building IDs, GUIDs and schema 16 remain unchanged.

### Manufactorum production and depot configuration

Selecting a Manufactorum opens a production popup with its tier, jobs, upkeep, staffing, network power, logistics, emissions, inventory, incoming cargo, line status and effective output. Four dropdowns select distinct products or disable a line. Tier I supplies one recipe cycle per five effective work seconds in total: one selected product receives 100%, two receive 50% each, three 33.3%, and four 25%. Existing tier/focus, worker and power modifiers apply to that shared budget. Disabled lines receive no work; an input-starved or full line retains its share without speeding other lines up or banking unlimited work. The popup temporarily pauses simulation; Close or Escape restores the prior pause state. The inspector's **SELECT PRODUCTION** button reopens it.

The current product catalog uses the existing material IDs: components (1 iron + 1 stone + 0.5 biomass), refined metal (2 iron + 1 biomass), plasteel (2 iron + 1 promethium) and synthetic crystals (2 stone + 1 promethium). Each completed recipe makes one unit and consumes all inputs atomically. Components remain the default for new and migrated Manufactorums. The buffer slider sets 1–120 units **per output material**, default 120. Full buffers halt that line and collection resumes it automatically. Recipe changes and lower limits retain existing stock. Unselected output stock can still be collected. Manufactorum components remain available for construction and depot storage; the historical Works-to-core currency export is retired; material remains in physical inventories.

Selecting a Storage Depot opens its statistics and storage popup. Each depot physically holds **1,200 units total across all materials**. **General** uses first-come, first-served reservations in deterministic cargo-dispatch order. **Designated** uses the item dropdown and quantity slider to reserve a separate amount for each of the nine materials; allocations cannot exceed 1,200 total, and zero excludes that item. Unassigned capacity stays unused in Designated mode. Switching modes retains the saved item reservations. Direct processing demand takes precedence over surplus storage; depots supply connected consumers and do not shuttle stock between depots. Stockpiles and cargo already dispatched are retained when changing modes or reducing limits, and receipts stop until the retained surplus drains. Incoming shipments reserve both the item's limit and the shared total before dispatch.

Depot capacity no longer increases every building's internal buffer. Existing over-limit inventories and incoming cargo remain intact and drain through normal use; they receive no further stock above the applicable limit. Schema 12 gains additive industry `facilityVersion: 1` configuration, per-line work and storage limits. Snapshots without that version migrate to components-only Manufactorums with their exact previous partial work, 120-unit output buffers and General depots, retaining all inventories/cargo. Malformed new settings are rejected before replacing a colony. Building IDs, material indices, asset/scene GUIDs and the enabled playable scene remain unchanged. Runtime HUD construction wires the popups in `WH30K_VS01`; new scripts include metadata.

## Solid mineral pits and haulage

Newly generated iron, copper, crystal and stone bodies use one expanding open pit. Surveyed major radius × minor radius × depth equals 100 times the recoverable reserve in gameplay units: doubling the reserve doubles the body's volume envelope, while linear dimensions scale by the cube root. Mineral type supplies the aspect ratios; small orbital veins follow the same rule with a shallower aspect. This describes compressed game geology, not physical tonnage or an exact excavated-mass model.

Clicking a mineral vein with Mine or Quarry selected previews the building on dry ground outside the **full surveyed extent**, including its footprint and a safety margin. The building stays at that permanent rim site as excavation advances. Other construction and adaptive expansion cannot occupy a revealed new mineral body, including an exhausted pit. The existing 250 m extraction reach still applies. Narrow-vein clearance is conservative along oblique edges. The inspector and Excavation Plan retain the same runtime wiring in `WH30K_VS01`; new solid pits have no sector-limit controls or expansion-decision interruptions. Their geometric ceiling is the mineral vein, and complete extraction reaches that ceiling. Mines continue inspecting their nearest matching deposit after exhaustion rather than silently switching bodies.

Removed quantity drives a widening, descending excavation horizon over fixed four-metre benches and a two-turn haul ramp. Every cut only lowers terrain; exposing a new part of the ramp cannot refill old workings. The actual spherical heightfield, terrain refreshes, pit meshes, placement and camera all use this cut. A ground-following approach connects the rim facility around the current lip to the exposed ramp. The road mesh includes paired dark wheel tracks. Two procedural six-wheel tipper vehicles shuttle between the entrance and working floor on separate lanes; their positions project onto a rebuilt route as the pit develops. Vehicles use simulated time and stop with pause, suspension, lost staffing/power, full local storage or exhaustion. Flooded routes hide/park the vehicles; extraction accounting remains the existing prototype abstraction. Biomass, water and liquid promethium do not create pits.

The additive per-deposit `layoutVersion`, `accessAngle` and floating-point extraction carry persist through schema 12's existing excavation state. Reserve arrays, depletion, geometry and access alignment are captured without aliasing. The carry conserves ore across thousands of small cycles. Road meshes and cosmetic vehicle travel phases rebuild after load; no vehicle can grant extra material. Existing saved geology defaults to layout zero and **retains its buildings, dimensions, directional limits, foundation pillars and unanswered mining decisions**. It is not silently resized or relocated. New geology receives the new solid-pit behavior; starting a new expedition provides it throughout the world. Scene/rendering acceptance for this change is pending; see verification notes.

## Legacy ore bodies, excavation and neighbouring facilities

Schema 6 stores eight directional ore reserves, extracted quantities, four-metre working benches, protected directions, foundation elevations and unanswered expansion decisions. Buildings constructed on an excavated floor retain their height on load. Iron is an elongated banded lens (50–65% estimated grade; 1,800–5,000 recoverable gameplay lots), copper a broader disseminated body (0.3–1.2%; 900–3,000 concentrated ore lots), crystals a narrower hydrothermal vein (350–1,200 lots), and stone a bedded deposit (3,500–9,000 lots). These are deliberately compressed game deposits, not real-world tonnage predictions. Grades describe host material; production lots represent recoverable ore/concentrate, preserving the existing factory recipes. Geological references: [Geoscience Australia iron](https://www.ga.gov.au/education/minerals-energy/australian-mineral-facts/iron) and [USGS porphyry copper model](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2010/5070/b/pdf/SIR10-5070B.pdf).

Extraction widens and deepens working sectors, modifying the same spherical heightfield used by placement and camera clearance. Local LOD meshes refresh and a dense, banded pit surface shows benches. Vegetation is removed from worked ground. Existing foundation pillars remain; flooded cuts follow the global ocean level. Exhausted deposits retain their pits. Ore does not regenerate, and a stopped mine keeps ore physically in the ground.

Before a cut reaches another building's footprint plus a 12-metre safety margin, mining raises a persistent decision and pauses simulated time. Demolish the threatened player-built facility, or limit that working direction. Demolition removes its economic contribution, inventory and linked in-flight cargo without a refund; all other inventories and cargo endpoints are remapped. Core facilities require a directional limit. The next cut checks every remaining neighbour again. Narrative choices and mining choices retain separate pause state and cannot overwrite each other's callbacks.

Select a mine or quarry, then **Excavation Plan** to review reserves, grade, depth and individual limits. Eight sector buttons can restrict or reopen directions; reopening checks obstructions again before extraction. **Inspect Cuts on Planet** shows lettered planning outlines (gold open, red limited) with the survey overlay. Blocking directions lowers accessible ore without deleting remaining geological reserves. The mine's own foundation is retained on a pillar. Biomass harvesting does not excavate a pit.

Version-5 colonies preserve deposit IDs, remaining quantities, inventories, cargo, buildings and terrain. Their original 2,000-unit capacities migrate into directional reserves with proportional historical excavation; newer geological sizes apply to new worlds. Older schemas retain their existing migration paths. Underground tunnels, geotechnical slope stability, drilling uncertainty, waste/tailings transport and reclamation are outside this prototype.

## Runtime performance

The October 28 reliability pass keeps local camera directions exact when testing cached views, so small moves on continental worlds render immediately. Dozer headings are calculated in metres before normalization, preserving valid movement and save state; stationary dozers reuse their terrain support samples. Session replacement closes old research, guide, discovery, mining and facility panels before restoring the new session's pause state. Guide navigation occupies the card footer, and compact catalog/command labels fit their buttons.

For existing large-world schema-10-16 saves, an exactly zero dozer heading receives a specific compatibility repair toward its saved destination or a stable parked tangent. Other malformed data still rejects; no stocks, population or command progress are reset. Depot, groundwater/desalination pump and Cognitorium geometry stays within its declared placement footprint, with the same geometry used in previews and built facilities. No building ID, scene GUID or save schema changes are required.

Tree cells scale with planet radius to retain local spacing. A completed ecology tile retains at most 1,681 tree instances, with at most two tiles during incremental replacement; overlapping tiles reuse existing objects, and retired trees are released. Cell identity uses the complete grid address. Tree cards have front/back faces and an explicit Resources cutout material for player shader retention. Farm land claims project facility-relative polygons instead of rounding every corner through million-metre world coordinates. Logistics connectivity remains authoritative while its decorative Routes mesh is built only when visible; it uses 32-bit indices and does not cast shadows. See the latest verification entry for the checks actually completed.

Terrain patches explicitly release owned meshes when merged or regenerated, including edit-mode comparison previews that never receive Play Mode lifecycle callbacks. The comparison window also releases its remaining patch meshes when closed. This changes resource ownership and counters without changing height samples or saved terrain recipes.

Residential background planning shares one 128-attempt slice per frame across a round-robin queue, discarding stale searches when geometry or terrain changes. It retains only the best distinct locations per direction and reuses validation scratch space. Colony collision volumes and developed bounds are cached, avoiding repeated neighbour mesh traversal and rebuilding. Completed module objects/foundations/colliders survive growth; only additions and roofs whose coverage changes are rebuilt, and construction work-plane updates reuse their buffers. The pure .NET allocation benchmark fell from 662.55 MiB to 57.20 MiB for three six-addition sequences (about 91% less). This does not establish Unity frame rates; actual Play Mode profiling remains pending the Editor licensing failure.

Building registries and operation effects are cached until state changes; shared architecture materials support GPU instancing. Terrain refinement is limited to four new patches per update, with visible parents retained until children are ready. Distant roots use fewer vertices; nested power-of-two cell grids share samples and deep skirts seal unequal LOD edges. Local vegetation and detailed water rebuild over multiple frames. Cargo reuses pooled visuals and cached sampled arcs, with bounded route caching. Survey presentation refreshes at 10 Hz, while camera and sun work skip unchanged state. Dispatch caches consumers and incoming reservations. The current save schema is 16; earlier save migrations are retained.

## Colony water supply

New colonies begin with 600 water, scaled by difficulty, in a separate finite fresh-water reserve capped at 5,000. Population consumes 0.05 water per simulation second plus 0.0001 per colonist per second. Low reserves slow growth; an empty reserve causes population decline. The top banner reports current water and months at present demand. Water pollution remains a separate planetary condition.

Build a **Groundwater Pump** on dry land to draw 1.2 water/s, or a **Desalination Pump** to produce 0.9 water/s. Both use workers and connected network power; output scales with staffing, available power, upgrades and building focus. Desalination must be placed on dry land with an ocean shoreline within 140 m. Pumps are in the **Utilities** construction page. Water is stored colony-wide rather than in facility cargo inventories. Schema 11 records the reserve explicitly; schemas 0–10 receive the difficulty-scaled starting reserve when loaded, while a saved zero reserve remains empty.

See [the performance review](PERFORMANCE_REVIEW.md) for measured editor results, tradeoffs, reproduction instructions and remaining scale limits. The short editor benchmark does not establish standalone target frame rates.

## Food, farming and fertilizer

The **FOOD** construction page supplies a **Colony Farm** (stable ID `Farm`): 8 construction components and 16 base jobs. Standard technology begins with one barn, silo/service buildings and five cultivated plots, producing **6,000 food per calendar month** at full staffing, connected power and basic fertilizer. A colonist needs **1 food/month**, consumed continuously against the shared 150-second month. New Explorator expeditions receive **120,000 food**; legacy three-building starts and pre-food migrations retain **960 food**, scaled by difficulty. Shared storage capacity is now 140,000; loading never refills an existing food reserve. Low food reserves slow growth before exhaustion; empty food or water causes decline. The output explanation names the shortage and remedy.

Operational focus selects the highest permitted fertilizer grade: basic **100%**, intermediate **125%**, advanced **150%**. Farms fall back to a supplied lower grade. These are complete formulations: grades replace each other rather than stacking. A base farm consumes **6 fertilizer and 1 manufactured component/month** at full operation. Power demand is 6 MW. Missing fertilizer/components, staffing, power or food-storage room proportionally limits food and input use; suspension stops production and operating costs while colony food demand continues. Physical components and fertilizer must arrive through the existing cargo network. The inspector shows actual estimated monthly output, local grade stocks, staffing/power, costs and consecutive growth progress; the HUD shows food/demand and all three fertilizer totals.

Residential blocks and the colony ship reclaim basic fertilizer from occupied housing (0.06 units/resident/month), while industry and the scientific survey outpost reclaim 0.12 units/staffed job/month. This represents sorted biological and recoverable nutrient waste, rather than every industrial effluent being usable on crops. Production scales with staffing; suspended sources stop. Player buildings store waste locally for existing cargo haulers. Founding buildings lack a player cargo index, so their waste is collected into the nearest connected active farm, configured processor or depot with room. Uncollected overflow is discarded. Habitation, factories and outposts participate in the proximity logistics graph for waste collection.

The existing **Manufactorum** product dropdowns append two recipes without changing the four original product IDs: **intermediate compost blend** consumes 2 basic reclaimed waste + 1 biomass; **advanced mineral nutrient** consumes 1 intermediate blend + 0.5 stone + 0.25 crystal + 0.25 promethium. Stone/crystals represent recovered mineral nutrients and promethium supplies processing chemistry. Each atomic cycle makes one unit; the existing shared work budget, buffers, depot reservations and physical cargo apply. Original nine material indices remain stable; fertilizer occupies indices 9�11.

Six **consecutive calendar months** at full operation add one field automatically. Enhanced fertilizer output also qualifies. Shortages or suspension interrupt the streak; pause preserves it. A field adds **20% of the original base food, jobs, component upkeep and fertilizer demand**, without compounding. Fields now form a connected patchwork of seeded irregular polygons with shared boundaries, varied areas/oblique corners and crop rows clipped to each shape. Narrow headlands shrink modestly as a cluster fills, increasing cultivated area inside stable parcel boundaries. Free outer boundaries vary by plot; neighbouring barn clusters meet along shared edges rather than leaving separated rectangular pads. Support-building milestones are four times their original added-field thresholds: the second building appears at 24 added fields (29 total), the third at 64, and the fourth at 104. Field patches still contain ten adjoining parcels, but are independent of support buildings; ordinary patches cultivate their centre instead of leaving unused barn courtyards. Expansion fills a connected sequence of compact 2x2 patch blocks. Only sites for the four planned support buildings reserve a courtyard. New fields and buildings require dry, gentle terrain clear of buildings, reserved residential volumes, the dozer and mineral bodies. Qualified farms wait for space and retry at bounded intervals. Developed fields protect their occupied footprint from future construction/earthworks. The prototype bounds a farm to 120 added fields (125 total); future farm technology levels are explicitly left as an extension comment in `FarmRules`.

In the **Unity Editor**, select a farm and click **DEV: ADD FIELD**. Each click immediately adds exactly one field, updates production/upkeep/jobs, resets the growth streak, refreshes terrain-following geometry and creates a satellite barn at its threshold. This explicit development override bypasses both the six-month requirement and land clearance; it can overlap obstructions. It shares the 120-field safety limit and is excluded from player compilation. Normal automatic growth retains clearance checks.

The founding barn retains its silo/service buildings. Its satellites have distinct silhouettes: an L-wing storehouse with a loading canopy, a tall granary with twin bins and transfer galleries, and a long three-bay machinery workshop with roof monitors, vents and a water tank. Designs are fixed by milestone and have restrained saved-seed height variation; the same barn mesh is not repeated. All use the existing photographic limestone, weathered iron, red roofing and brass material system. These are visual support milestones: the existing per-field economic effects remain authoritative. The inspector names the next design and counts down its remaining fields. Soil and planted rows follow the shared spherical terrain heightfield. Placement previews, built geometry and growth clearance use the same parcel layout. Each farm saves an additive `layoutSeed`; pre-seed farms derive it once from their saved planetary position, keeping their field count, productive months and economy. The schema remains 13. Existing saves retain their field count, seed, growth time, inventory and economic effects; the farm presentation rebuilds with the new sparse support-building plan, consolidating earlier decorative repeated barns and filling unused courtyards. Meshes remain modular; photographic surfaces and both target-resolution scene renders have been reviewed as recorded in verification notes; modular geometry remains a prototype limit. The existing playable scene discovers the new Resources definition and runtime UI without changing scene GUIDs. Schema **13** records food/version and farm count/progress; industry material version 2 appends fertilizer inventories/statistics and extends depot allocations. Schemas 0�12 preserve their existing stocks, cargo, products, settings and migrations, receiving empty fertilizer slots and a finite initial food reserve. A saved schema-13 zero-food reserve remains zero.

## Planetary forests and timber

### Photographic tree assets

Forest and initial tree placement remains procedural and seed-stable.
