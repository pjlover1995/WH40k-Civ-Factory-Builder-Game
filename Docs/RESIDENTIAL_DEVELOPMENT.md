> Current economy (2026-10-29): residential construction spends physical components. Historical project price/paid fields and library cost values retain their original units for compatibility; multiply by 0.01 for physical component costs. No currency income or upkeep is charged. Work depends on staffing, power and services. Existing paid progress remains paid after loading. The current project brief governs these rules.

# Residential development

`HabBlock` remains the building/save ID. Its displayed name is **Residential Block**. The core and player-built blocks use the same runtime components in `WH30K_VS01`; `ManagedBuilding.Configure` wires them automatically. The existing playable scene is already enabled in Build Settings. No hand-added scene component is required.

## Playing

Select a block or one of its completed modules. Scroll the inspector details with the mouse wheel. The inspector shows block housing/jobs/upkeep, colony provision and unmet need for each class, the preferred option and its reasoning, or the reserved project's paid credits, work and waiting reason. A module click additionally reports its class, delivered capacity, role, parent connection and floor. **Whole Block Details** clears that module detail without losing block controls.

**Development: Automatic / Manual** controls approvals. Automatic blocks commission one module at a time when there is class demand and the full quoted credit budget is currently available. In Manual, **Approve** commissions the currently preferred candidate. Both modes validate the chosen candidate against current demand, funding and site rules before reserving anything. Approval reuses the displayed candidate without repeating the full search. **Prefer: Balanced / Upward / Outward** adjusts scoring; preference cannot bypass physical rules. **Suspend / Resume** pauses/resumes work and the building's operating economy. Existing focus controls still apply.

The pale-yellow continuous outline follows the union of completed occupied tiles, including galleries, concave wings and courtyard holes. Dashed faint outlines are suggestions, brighter yellow marks the preferred option, amber marks an unfunded option, and a vertical line/ghost floor marks supported upward potential. At most five options are shown, including both ground and upper options when both exist. Suggestions reserve no land. Only an approved project reserves its actual occupied columns. Other buildings' placement checks use those columns after a broad distance check.

A block develops both ground wings and upper floors over successive projects. Balanced targets a 1:1 mix, Upward approximately 2:1 upper/ground, and Outward approximately 1:2. These are scoring targets rather than constraints: when the preferred direction cannot fit, another legal direction can develop. All completed load-bearing wings remain eligible for supported upper floors. Existing completed positions are preserved. Habitation capacity increases exactly once on completion of either kind of addition, and the pending project reports its focused housing increase; circulation-only galleries add no housing.

New ground residences prefer a configurable two-tile gap, joined to the parent entrance by a narrow covered gallery with its own terrain supports. The default separation is roughly 8–10 metres depending on the existing building scale. A one-tile gap or compact infill is available when the larger gap cannot fit. New residence volumes keep a two-metre clearance from other facilities. Upper additions still require actual continuous support and do not create airborne spaced wings.

An obstructed approved project automatically searches all valid directions and supported surfaces for the **same design**. Its locked cost/duration, paid credits, work, class housing and legacy investment are retained, even if current housing demand or credits are zero. Replanning changes the pending reservation only; it does not move completed buildings. If nothing fits, work and payment pause and the search retries after a geometry change or at most every two real seconds. Suspension also pauses redirection. The inspector reports successful redirections.

Pending architecture stays at its chosen position and dimensions, translucent amber, while its work plane rises. Completed work replaces the ghost once; structures do not slide out of the founding building or inflate during construction. Deselecting hides the planning display.

## Library and physical rules

`Assets/Resources/ResidentialModules.json` configures **20 worker, 20 administration and 20 leadership habitation designs**. Stable IDs are `worker-01` through `worker-20`, `admin-01` through `admin-20`, and `leader-01` through `leader-20`. Their footprint patterns and height profiles produce 60 distinct volumes after accounting for rotation/translation. They include courts, galleries, meeting wings, reception residences and communal facilities. Each design has its own configured wall/roof/trim palette. Five roof profiles (parapet terrace, gabled bays, hipped pavilions, sawtooth clerestories and stepped roof garden) combine with four facade treatments per class. Shared stone/iron/brass textures now receive explicit UVs, and separated roof/ceiling planes prevent coplanar surface speckling. A deterministic serial-based tint variation differentiates repeated copies without altering saved footprints. Materials and meshes remain shared/cached. Stone, iron, oxide roofs, brass pointed window surrounds, enclosed/open galleries and distributed circulation shafts retain the game's gothic industrial language. `res-core` is the mixed founding residence; `res-link` is an additional small connecting gallery/service riser, excluded from the sixty habitation designs.

A column occupies a 0.2 × 0.2 tile in normalized building coordinates. One usable level is 0.12 high; world scale remains the building's existing transform dimensions. A column's integer height describes its complete occupied vertical interval. Every exposed edge has a centred entrance at the module's entrance floor. Circulation shafts reach the column's occupied levels and accessible terrace. Definitions configure class, capacity, role, columns/heights, roof, upper-support/lift permissions, enclosure preference, service need, credit cost and work duration. Do not rename IDs or change the geometry of already deployed definitions without a layout migration.

Each decision evaluates four rotated module variants against cardinal entrance anchors and actual roof tops, favouring recent completed modules within a 24,000-attempt budget. Both ends of each exposed cardinal edge and gap sizes 2/1/0 are considered. Shared infill galleries can fill a one-tile gap with at least two neighbouring floor entrances. Validation rejects occupied column intervals, unmatched entrance levels, missing parents, unsupported upper columns, missing roof/lift permissions, out-of-limit geometry, terrain intrusions, excessive pile depth, other buildings' volumes, service causeways and geological excavation envelopes. Upward floors require **every** occupied column to stand on a completed, permitted support at exactly that elevation. Spires and mesh peaks are never support measurements.

Terrain checks sample nine points per ground tile from the actual spherical, excavation-aware heightfield. The founding deck clears its sampled footprint; each completed ground addition gets its own ground-reaching foundations. Terrain revisions invalidate site caches and refresh changed foundations. Other residential blocks are checked by individual columns; nonresidential obstacles use their declared body volumes and actual adaptive mesh bounds. Pending nonresidential campus work conservatively reserves its existing full expansion bounds until completion. These obstacles are conservative reservations rather than a triangle-level navigation/physics solver. The existing excavation protection also sees the developed/reserved block bounds. New residential foundations avoid mineral bodies' major extent plus the configurable safety clearance, including before excavation begins.

## Population and economy

The classes are a partition of the existing population: **78% workers, 18% administration, 4% leadership** by default. They create neither additional residents nor jobs. Existing global workforce allocation remains unchanged. Founding residential capacity and nonresidential housing split by these shares. Completed habitation modules contribute their saved class/capacity once. Focus affects module housing by the existing 1.4/0.85 multipliers; legacy tier multipliers apply only to the retained founding economy. Suspending a block removes all of its provision.

Desired total residents = `max(population × 1.12, min(population × 1.5, active jobs / 0.62 × habitability))`. Split that target by class, subtract colony-wide provision, clamp each shortfall to zero. The 0.62 matches the settlement's current workforce ratio. Population headroom and staffing opportunity justify growth without a separate social-mobility simulation. No project provides housing until completion. Additional modules add housing and configurable upkeep (0.0125 credits per base resident per second); they do not duplicate founding jobs, production or environmental effects.

Construction remains **credits only**. It does not consume iron, components or other industry inventory. Each design's cost/work and library credit/work multipliers are explicit. Work spends credits continuously and waits safely if money, site validity or labour is unavailable. The full budget is required at initial approval, but only completed work is charged, so later shortages can pause a project. Paid credits determine work exactly, avoiding accumulated rounding stranding a nearly completed project. A saved quarter-second work accumulator prevents save/load from losing deferred construction time. New quotes include the gallery's configurable 12 credits and six work seconds per gap tile, affected by the existing multipliers. A redirected project honours its already approved contract; a longer replacement connection does not charge that work again.

Construction speed is the minimum of actual global staffing fraction, actual settlement production/upkeep ratio (bounded to one), and service access. An active utility with jobs within 250 metres gives full service access; otherwise the configurable basic service factor is 0.35. That factor is reduced by actual **global** generated-power/demand fraction when there is grid demand. This is a deliberately small service-access model, not a measurement of local grid voltage or individual household consumption. Utility suspension, employment, production, power and credit shortages therefore have real effects on development.

Scoring uses class shortfall/capacity, credit cost, compatible class neighbours, service access, plan preference, usable spacing, height and a seed/sequence tie-break. Unused designs get a bonus; repeated and immediately repeated designs get penalties. Physically valid alternatives therefore produce visible architectural variety. A deficit against the preference's target mix favours the next available upper floor or ground wing; spacing remains preferred for ground additions. CO2, water pollution, habitability and temperature are actual **global** environmental inputs. Harsher conditions favour enclosed modules; mild conditions favour open communal galleries. There are no invented local pollution, wind, noise or sunlight fields. Terrain and nearby utility distance are the local measurements.

## Saves and migration

The schema-9 operation gains additive `residential` state with its own version marker. It stores block identity/seed/sequence, ordered module IDs, ownership, serials, placements/rotations/floors, parent connections, completion flags, delivered class/capacity, approved placement, locked cost/duration, paid credits/work, legacy investment and deferred time. Additive straight-gallery coordinates/length/direction and project redirection count preserve the new layout. Older placements default to zero gap and remain valid at their existing positions. Narrow passages occupy validated clearance tiles but cannot support upper residences. Existing operation fields retain manual mode, preference, focus and suspension. Derived meshes, occupancy, terrain and preview caches are rebuilt; previewing never changes generation decisions.

Older `HabBlock` saves deliberately consolidate completed legacy tier investment into the founding building's existing footprint/economy. Their old radial annexes and peak-mounted crowns are **not** also rendered. Housing, jobs, upkeep, focus and economic investment remain the original tier values. The visual limitation is that historical rings are not reconstructed as individual apartments: this avoids installing unvalidated buildings inside neighbours.

A legacy pending improvement retains its original cost, work and inferred already-paid amount (`cost × work / duration`). It seeks a currently legal replacement module through the same validator. If none fits, its investment waits in the save. Completion charges only the remaining historical cost and delivers the original next economic tier once. Its physical replacement module is marked as housing already credited to the founding tier, so it cannot add housing/upkeep a second time. The inspector identifies that accounting. Historical direction/ring layouts are replaced rather than geometrically reproduced.

Save validation checks ownership, bounded fields, serial order, IDs, class allocation, payment/work consistency, then replays geometry to reject overlap, invalid connections and unsupported placements. Unity-style all-empty inline class instances normalize as absent legacy state or an absent project, without discarding real paid work. Named surviving shared meshes/materials can repopulate caches after a domain reload. Terrain/other-building changes trigger a search for another valid site for an already approved design. Paid work and funds remain unchanged; when no alternative fits the project waits rather than charging or delivering housing.

## Bounds and remaining acceptance

Default limits are 32 completed pieces including the core/galleries, 12 tiles from the origin and 12 levels. A search considers at most 24,000 habitation options plus bounded connecting infill. Automatic searches share one 128-attempt slice per rendered frame across a round-robin queue; publication is atomic when a search finishes. Two seconds after completion they may refresh demand/environment inputs. Manual unselected blocks do not continuously search. Terrain/geometry changes cancel unfinished searches and clear site caches before payment or another reservation. Explicit fresh-search calls and obstruction rerouting remain synchronous; approval normally reuses the displayed option and performs live validation.

Validation reuses scratch cell lists and indexed design/parent data. Ranking keeps at most five distinct locations per direction and formats explanations only for displayed choices. Colony collision snapshots (world broad bounds and oriented boxes) rebuild on geometry or terrain revision, while developed/reserved block bounds are cached. These caches include paid reservations and preserve full 3-D collision tests, terrain-reaching foundations and causeway checks. Completed module objects/colliders/foundations survive reservation and unrelated completion; only new modules, terrain-affected foundations and roofs whose coverage changes are rebuilt. Work-plane bounds, renderer and point buffer are reused. A forced restore/domain reload reconstructs derived visuals from the unchanged save state.

Quarter-second simulated construction steps, shared meshes/materials, a 512-entry mesh cache and batched suggestion geometry remain. `PlanningSearches`, `ModuleObjectsBuilt` and `ResidentialVolumeBuilds` are diagnostic counters for the Unity checks/profiler. The repeatable pure-rules benchmark is `dotnet run --project Tests/Simulation/SimulationChecks.csproj -c Release -- . --residential-benchmark`: three six-addition sequences after warmup, seed 1234, all classes demanded and fully funded, without scene collision or rendering. Managed allocation fell from 662.55 MiB before these changes to 57.20 MiB (about 91% less). The current warmed Release sample took 622.1 ms for all eighteen planning/completion calls; JIT warmup and machine load affect timing. This is not a frame-rate or large-colony performance guarantee.

The implementation has engine-independent and Unity-reference verification. Actual Editor import, Play Mode scene checks, domain-reload recovery, rendering/selection readability at both target resolutions, uneven-terrain foundation review, long-session performance and a standalone player remain unverified because the installed Editor's Licensing Client timed out.
